The Wordmark

The name already contains the idea. Set it so people see it.

TRACK carries the weight; con and tor step back. The rule underneath is segmented rather than solid, so the wordmark carries the same tread language as the marks instead of looking like a default underline. This works with any of the five below, or on its own.

Where it's weakSet in Source Sans Black, which is what I could render and check here — the network blocks the font sources I'd actually choose. Treat the letterforms as placeholder and the structure as the proposal; a sturdier grotesque like Archivo would sharpen it.

01

Tread C

The name's first letter, cut into tread.

The only direction that is a letter and a track at the same time — it carries the name without spelling it, which is what a monogram is for. Cut into seven segments so it reads as tread rather than a plain ring.

Where it's weakThe radial gaps can read as a loading spinner at a glance. Tightening the segment count would fix it.

Framing Square

The tool every one of them already owns.

A steel square with its graduations knocked out. Measurement is the whole idea — the marks along the arm say tracking without a chart or a graph in sight. The most trade-native of the five.

Where it's weakIt says builder more than it says conTRACKtor. Nothing in the form ties back to the name.

Track Check

Captured, and confirmed.

The product's actual moment — Approve All, then Saved. The tail is laid out as track segments so the check and the tracking are one gesture. The most energetic mark here.

Where it's weakThe weakest silhouette of the five. It survives 20px but starts to close up at 16.

Step Climb

Job to job, up.

Stairs read as both framing and growth, and the notches keep it from being a bar chart. Structurally the most durable mark on the sheet — it loses almost nothing at favicon size.

Where it's weakAlso the most generic. Rotate the palette and it could belong to any finance app.

Receipt Track

The paperwork it does for you.

A docket with a tread strip where the line items go. Literal to the promise on the tin, and the clearest of the five at small sizes by a wide margin.
